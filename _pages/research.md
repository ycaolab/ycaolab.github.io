# Research

Our goal is to explain how the brain selects what to process when evidence is noisy, incomplete, or costly. We start from a normative question: **given limited capacity, which information should an agent sample, and for which objective?** We formalize the answers as sampling policies, fit them to behavior with computational models, and test their predictions in neural population representations measured with MEG and EEG.

In natural environments, information sampling takes place in a multidimensional space. We study two of its dimensions, decision alternatives and sensory modalities. An overarching level, "sampling policies and objectives", develops the theory that explains both.

**2. Sampling across sensory modalities**
{: .level-title}

Which sources to combine. Sensory signals should be integrated when they share a cause and kept apart when they do not. We ask how the brain makes this flexible decision, and how it unfolds in neural population representations within single trials, measured with MEG at millisecond resolution.

**3. Sampling policies, attention, and objectives**
{: .level-title}

Which information to seek, and when. Both dimensions above are instances of one problem: an agent with limited capacity must decide what to sample to serve an objective. We ask which policies solve this problem, how attention moves across alternatives over time, and how the objective, reward seeking or information seeking, changes the policy. Competing policies make different predictions for behavior and for neural population representations, so the data can tell them apart.

## Facilities and networks in Magdeburg

The lab is part of the Institute of Psychology at Otto-von-Guericke University. We are embedded in the Magdeburg neuroscience community and benefit from its shared infrastructure.

**Within the Institute of Psychology.** Our lab and the General Psychology division host EEG (BioSemi) and multiple eye tracking (EyeLink) systems for behavioral and electrophysiological experiments.

**MEG.** The lab can run MEG experiments in Magdeburg together with local collaborators. MEG has millisecond resolution, which suits questions about attention sampling and decision dynamics.

**Functional MRI.** Several scanners at 3T and 7T are available across the OvGU campus. The lab and the General Psychology division have frequent access to them through local collaborations.

**Networks.** Magdeburg hosts a dense network of neuroscience research, including the Center for Behavioral Brain Sciences (CBBS) and the German Center for Neurodegenerative Diseases (DZNE), as well as larger consortia such as the CRC 1436 on Neural Resources of Cognition and the Research Training Group 2413 on the Aging Synapse. The lab is connected to this community through the General Psychology division, whose members take part in CBBS and CRC 1436, and we are building further links.
